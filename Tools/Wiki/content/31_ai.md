## Fair play

- The AI plays by **the same rules** as you: the same income and cap, one unlock and two squads a round (plus any slots it buys), the same draft, cards and towers.
- It **never sees more than you would**: its own state, both bases, last round's base damage, your faction and starting squads after the draft, and your army (types, levels, positions, techs, items, buffs, tower levels) **as it was when the last battle started**. It never sees your new squads or your spells until a battle reveals them.
- Difficulty changes **how well it decides**, nothing else.

## How it plays a round

1. It looks at your last revealed army and finds the role you spent most on.
2. It picks a **strategy**: Swarm (chaff, flankers), Armor Push (tanks, single-target DPS, Titans), Air Raid (aircraft, some anti-air) or Balanced.
3. It **scores** every unit it can field (how well it suits the strategy, how well it counters you, a small bonus per tier, and, on Normal and Hard, a bonus for its sub-faction's exclusives), unlocks at most one type, and buys up to its squad limit.
4. It spends the rest on level-ups (its most valuable squads first), a tech for the type it has invested most in, then towers and research.
5. It places squads by role: chaff in front, tanks and Titans just behind, flankers on the side edges, chaff clear and DPS behind the tanks, anti-air and aircraft further back, artillery at the back.
6. **Cards:** it takes the reinforcement card that adds the most for its price, or the general card it values most while keeping a reserve, and skips when nothing beats the skip bonus. It aims strikes, zones, EMPs and smoke at the thickest part of your last revealed army, drop squads at your most valuable squad, and buffs on its own thickest group, and puts items and training on its best squads.
7. **Against the Flood**, Normal and Hard avoid infantry that can be infected, and want chaff clear and armor.

## Difficulty levels

| | Easy | Normal | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Draft** | A random card | The card whose free squads are worth most | Same as Normal |
| **Strategy** | Often random | Counters your **most expensive role** | Picks the strategy that does best against your **whole** army, and changes it after a round where it took more base damage than it dealt |
| **Unit choice** | Lots of randomness; stops buying about a third of the time | Counters by role | **Measured counters** from the matchup table, plus each unit's overall strength |
| **Upgrades** | Rarely levels up or buys techs | Levels up; buys the cheapest tech, drop techs first | Levels up; picks the tech worth most against your army, drop techs first |
| **Towers** | Ignores them | Levels the tower that has fallen most; researches with 300 to spare | Same with 200 to spare; buys oil; Elite Recruitment with 1,000+ credits; extra slot with 400+; Rapid Resupply when about to lose; **uses flank zones** and **defends flanked towers** |
| **Cards** | Skips half the time, otherwise takes a random card; forgets half its spells and aims at random | Takes the card worth most, keeping a reserve | Same with a smaller reserve, and weighs how well a reinforcement counters you |

## The Hard AI's counter table

Hard reads a **matchup table**: how often each unit beats each other unit in battles of equal value, measured by the game's own simulation. Single-unit armies of about 1,800 credits fight 10 battles per pair, with sides swapped. It buys what beats the part of your army that its own army doesn't beat yet, values strong all-round units, and avoids units your army beats.

The same table drives the **Good against · bad against** lists on every unit's page and the [counter web](#/counters).

> **Note:** In testing, Hard beats Normal about 43 to 45 times in 50, and Normal beats Easy 50 times in 50.
